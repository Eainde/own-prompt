Thanks for the response. To answer your question on why we've adopted this approach, here is the rationale behind the current design.

1. Data ownership and segregation
The KYC documents (passports, proof of address, certificates of incorporation, UBO registers, financial statements) belong to our application and sit in our segregated GCP project. That project is where data classification, retention policies, encryption and access controls for this data are owned and enforced. Keeping the documents there, and referencing them rather than moving them, keeps a single controlled copy under the data owner's governance.

2. No duplication of customer PII
Passing GCS links means the files are never copied or re-hosted elsewhere. Any alternative that moves the content (copying into another bucket, or embedding it in the request) creates additional copies or transient footprints of customer PII outside the owning project. That complicates retention, deletion and right-to-erasure obligations, and weakens data lineage.

3. Reduced exposure within our own services
With GCS links, the document bytes don't pass through our application pods, logs or traces. If we sent the content inline instead, every document would be downloaded, held in memory, encoded and transmitted through additional hops, which increases the surface where sensitive data could be logged or exposed.

4. Nature of KYC documents
A typical onboarding case, especially for corporate clients, involves multiple scanned, multi-page documents that together are often well beyond inline request size limits. The value of the AI feature comes from the model reviewing these documents together in a single request, for example cross-checking a director's name and date of birth on the UBO register against their passport. Referencing files by link is what makes that possible without splitting or down-sampling documents, which would reduce accuracy.

One concern I'd like to raise alongside this: if the direction is to route document content through the central/global project instead, that means customer KYC PII being processed in, and potentially transiting or logged in, a shared project outside our data boundary. Has the Compliance / Data Protection team reviewed and approved PII from segregated application projects being handled in the global project? It would be good to have that confirmed before we look at alternatives, as it may affect which options are viable for us.
